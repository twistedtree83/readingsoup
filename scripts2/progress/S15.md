# S15 — Accommodation cards: dealing, play, resolution, attribution

Issue: #38 · Branch: `main` · Status: **shipped**

## What shipped

The diagnostic half of the activity, in a room. When a spotlight starts,
everybody except the reader is dealt a hand. Play a card and either the barrier
comes off the reader's screen — named on the projector — or nothing happens and
the card is spent.

- **Three cards each at six or more active participants, the whole deck at two
  to five.** Scarcity is what makes cards interesting in a large group; in a
  small one it just leaves the round with no way out.
- **Somebody always holds the card that lifts the barrier**, guaranteed by
  construction rather than left to chance.
- **Observers are dealt in**, on identical terms.
- **The correct card re-renders the passage server-side** and sends a fresh
  token array. The client never un-mangles anything.
- **A wrong card changes nothing and is spent**, visibly, in place.

## Decisions taken

- **The guarantee is a repair, not a retry.** Hands are drawn from a seeded
  shuffle; if no hand contains the fix, one recipient is chosen at random and a
  card they hold is displaced by it. At three from six the odds of nobody
  holding it are one in two *per hand* — negligible at twenty people, a coin
  flip at six, where the round would have no way out at all. A barrier that
  cannot be lifted is the one outcome this activity must never produce.
- **Every hand is sorted into canonical deck order.** Draw order, or the slot
  the guaranteed card landed in, would let a player read something off the
  arrangement of their own cards.
- **Hand size keys off active participants, not headcount.** Observers hold a
  hand but do not push a small room into scarcity — they are not the reason a
  group is a group. This matches how the PRD defines its group-size modes;
  [[S19]] owns the modes themselves.
- **Spent is per round.** Hands are cleared at `END_ROUND` and dealt fresh: the
  next reader has a different barrier, and a carried-over hand would arrive
  already half spent against it.
- **A spent card stays on screen**, greyed and struck through rather than
  removed. What the room has already tried is worth seeing, and trying the
  wrong thing is the part worth remembering.
- **Solo is untouched.** One person holds the whole deck, wrong cards are not
  spent, attempts are unlimited. The reducer distinguishes the two by whether
  the round has a `reader` — there is no mode flag.
- **The reader holds nothing.** They are the one being helped, and a reader who
  could play their own way out would be diagnosing their own barrier.

## Attribution, and why it is lopsided

A correct play is named on the projector with the card: it is a generous act,
done in public, and the room should see who made the reading possible.

A wrong play is counted — *"One card played."* — and left anonymous. Being
generous and wrong about how to help a struggling colleague is not something to
put in projector-scale type at a staff meeting. The asymmetry is asserted
directly: after a wrong play, the player's name appears in no view at all.

This is also the concrete reason observers are dealt in. The half of the
activity that is visible and creditable is the half that carries no exposure —
an observer's correct play is named exactly like anyone else's, which makes the
role something a person might choose on its merits.

## Two adjustments this forced

- **The watching screen stops inverting once you hold cards.** Waiting is the
  task when your hands are empty, and the ink screen says so. With a hand it is
  not waiting at all — and two of the six cards are ink-grounded, so on an
  inverted screen they vanish into the background they were designed to sit
  against. Paper when dealt, ink when not.
- **The leak assertions now strip the card vocabulary before scanning.** One
  card is called *Change the colours*, and a passage uses the word "change", so
  a blunt substring scan reported a leak that was not one. Card names are a
  closed vocabulary that legitimately reaches every phone; removing them first
  keeps the check honest in both directions rather than loosening it.

## Known gap

At **one** active participant the table is empty, so nobody holds a card and
the reader cannot be helped. That is the solo-fallback case — a two-person
session with one observer — and [[S19]] owns it. In solo mode proper the reader
holds the deck themselves, which is already covered.

The **watch window and the room-wide play cap** are not here: cards are live the
moment they are dealt and there is no limit on how many are played. [[S16]] owns
both, and the PRD's copy correction about the locked state belongs with it.

## Verified

185 unit tests. The correct-holder guarantee is asserted over every condition
across a hundred deals, and end-to-end at 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 12 and 20 participants
across seven seeds; the 5/6 hand-size seam is pinned from both sides. Harness
green at 1, 2, 5, 8 and 20, including the on-the-wire proof: a room that plays
everything it holds always gets the barrier off, no card-holder ever receives
tokens, and a wrong play is counted without a name. In the browser: a full deck
on a watcher's phone, a wrong card struck through in place, *"One card played."*
on the projector with nobody named, and *"That helped — Aisha played Give it
room"* when the right one lands.
