# S20 — Reveal and debrief

Issue: #43 · Branch: `main` · Status: **shipped**

## What shipped

The end of the session, which until now did not exist: a room could run a whole
activity and then sit in the lobby.

**The projector shows six things you can do on Monday** — all six barriers, each
with what it feels like from the inside and the support that lifts it, in
projector-scale type. **Not one name on the screen.**

**Each phone privately marks that person's own barriers.** Three debrief
questions follow, advanced by hand, and the six stay on screen behind every one
of them.

## Decisions taken

- **The `had` mark is absent from the projector, not false.** A screen the whole
  room can see has no business carrying the field at all, so the catalogue
  builder takes a predicate and the host passes none. Asserted as
  `had === undefined` rather than as `had === false` — a falsy value is one
  refactor away from being a truthy one.
- **The questions sit above the six, never instead of them.** They are what the
  room is meant to leave with, and a question that replaced them would put the
  answer out of sight exactly while people are reaching for it. It also keeps
  the projector stateless: there is nothing to navigate back from.
- **The prompts go to the facilitator only.** They are said out loud; a phone
  full of the script would have twenty people reading ahead instead of talking.
  Asserted that no participant view carries `debrief` at all.
- **"Which student does this remind you of?" is deliberately last.** The first
  two questions are about the people in the room, and only once they have said
  what it felt like does the third land as recognition rather than as a lesson.
- **A room records what somebody met against their token; the solo tour records
  it against the tour.** The catalogue reads both, so the same screen serves a
  twenty-person reveal and a person who took the tour alone at their desk.
- **Advancing stops at the last question** rather than falling off the end, and
  the advance button disappears with it.

## Why there is no who-had-what slide

The PRD replaced it, and this slice is where that decision becomes concrete.
Such a list **cannot satisfy observer invisibility** — an observer is simply
absent from it, which is the disclosure the role exists to prevent — and it
would not fit on a projector at twenty people. Attribution instead happens by
**show of hands**: *hands up if you had Soup*. That makes disclosure voluntary
and gives anyone who would rather not take part in it an ordinary way to
decline.

An observer's phone shows the catalogue with nothing marked, and its shape is
asserted identical to a participant's — same keys, same six items — so it reads
as a catalogue that happens to have nothing ticked rather than as a different
screen.

## The reveal is complete however the session went

Every active participant met a barrier in the silent round, so the facilitator
can stop after two spotlights or after twelve and nobody comes up blank.
Asserted at twenty participants after **zero**, two, and the full eight
spotlight rounds: everyone active has something marked in all three.

One honest limit: that argument rests on the silent round, which is
group-only. In a **small** room (2–5) there is no silent round, so a facilitator
who abandons the session before its rounds have gone round could leave somebody
unmarked. The draw takes from whoever has read least, so it takes deliberate
early abandonment to reach — but it is not the same guarantee, and it is
recorded here rather than papered over.

## Verified

252 unit tests: the six with descriptions and matching accommodations; no
participant name and no `had` field on the projector; each phone marked with
exactly its own `seen` set and nothing else; at twenty people with three
observers, no view containing another participant's name, token, or barrier;
observers marked with nothing; completeness after 0, 2 and 8 spotlights; three
prompts advancing one at a time and stopping at the last.

Harness green at 1, 2, 5, 8 and 20, ending every session in the reveal: the
projector unnamed and unmarked, every phone holding six items and only its own
marks, and no phone receiving the facilitator's questions. In the browser: the
six laid out in a grid at 1440px with each card in its own colours, and *"Question
1 of 3 — What did you do when you got stuck?"* banded above them.
