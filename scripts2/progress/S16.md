# S16 — Round pacing: watch window, play cap, facilitator override

Issue: #39 · Branch: `main` · Status: **shipped**

## What shipped

The two rules that make the card game a diagnostic exercise rather than a race,
and the valve that stops either of them trapping a reader.

- **Watch-only window.** No card can be played for the first 25 seconds of a
  round. Every phone shows the countdown; the projector shows *"Cards down.
  Just watch — 12s."*
- **Three plays per round, room-wide.** Not three each. First come, each play
  spending that card from that player's hand.
- **Facilitator override.** A quiet link on the announce slide grants the
  accommodation directly, at any moment.

## Decisions taken

- **Authority from the play's own timestamp; the tick only drives the
  display.** Whether a card may be played is decided against `event.at` against
  the round's `startedAt`, not against whatever the last tick happened to
  write. A dropped tick can make a countdown stutter; it can never open the
  window early.
- **A card refused by the clock is not spent.** Rejected before the window, and
  rejected past the cap, leave the hand untouched — the player was not allowed
  to act, so nothing of theirs has been used up. Only a play that actually lands
  costs a card.
- **The window is per round, not per reader.** Somebody who has already sat
  through one still waits through the next: the noticing has to happen against
  *this* reader, and it is the most transferable moment in the session. You
  cannot accommodate what you have not noticed.
- **The cap counts the room.** Three plays between nineteen people is what makes
  somebody say *wait, don't waste it, listen to how she's saying it.* One rule
  fixes both failure modes — the large-group stampede and a small-group player
  working through all six cards alone.
- **The override lands through the same path as a correct play.** One `lift`
  function, whether a player found the card or the facilitator handed it over:
  the reader's experience of being helped must not depend on which.
- **The override names the card, not a person.** On a found card the player's
  name takes the big type; on a granted one the *card* does, and nobody is
  named. Whose plays did not land is not the room's business.
- **`DONE` is untouched by both rules.** Finishing never gets harder — asserted
  inside the locked window and again with every play burnt.
- **Solo has neither rule.** No window (it is self-paced, and there is nobody to
  watch) and no cap (unlimited attempts, no penalty). The reducer tells the two
  apart by whether the round has a `reader`, not by a mode flag.
- **The window length travels on `START_ROUND`**, defaulting to config, exactly
  as the silent round's duration travels on `START_SILENT`. That is what lets
  the harness run a *real* watch window in one second without the core learning
  anything about being under test.
- **The shell ticks twice a second** while anything is counting down, and not at
  all otherwise. A countdown that jumps two seconds at a time in front of a room
  reads as a broken clock.

## Copy correction from the PRD, now applied

The locked state says **cards unlock partway through the round** — *"Watch
first. Cards unlock in 12s."* — rather than implying they arrive when the reader
finishes. The whole mechanic is watch-then-act, and cards that turned up after
the reading would be watch-then-nothing.

## Verified

202 unit tests: a play one millisecond early is rejected and unspent while the
same play on the boundary lands; the window restarts for a second round with a
reader who has already watched one; three plays by three different people
exhaust a fourth person who has not played at all; the correct card is refused
once the plays are gone; the override resolves inside the window, past the cap,
and on a typed round; and solo is proved unaffected by either rule.

Harness green at 1, 2, 5, 8 and 20 with a real one-second window: a card thrown
at a locked round comes back unspent, the room runs out of plays rather than the
person, a fourth play is refused, and the override lifts the barrier with nobody
named. In the browser: the countdown ticking on the announce slide, *"That's
three. No more cards this round."*, and the granted slide carrying *Give it
room* in place of a name.

One run out of six failed at twenty participants with a stalled `END_ROUND`
while the machine was also running a browser session and two stray harness
processes; five consecutive runs since have been clean and the cause was not
reproducible. The loop now stops at that point instead of driving the next round
into a room still in the current one, so if it recurs it reports the one failure
that happened rather than four downstream ones.
