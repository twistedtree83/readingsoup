# S18 — True handovers: scribe and read it to them

Issue: #41 · Branch: `main` · Status: **shipped**

## What shipped

The two cards that hand you a **person** rather than deleting a difficulty.

**Give them a scribe.** The keyboard genuinely moves. The card player's phone
becomes the input, the reader's phone switches to *"Say it out loud"* with no
keyboard at all, and the room watches the sentence finally appear correctly —
produced by somebody else.

**Read it to them.** The card player's phone receives the clean passage, on one
phone in the room, and they read it aloud *to* the reader — whose own screen
stays exactly as hard as it was, because the help arrives through the air.

**The projector mirror.** A person silently typing gives the room no signal at
all, so the mangled output is mirrored at projector scale, character by
character, uncorrected. The target sentence is withheld until the round ends,
then appears beside what actually arrived.

## Decisions taken

- **Handovers do not call `lift()`.** Every other correct card removes the
  barrier from the environment; these two leave it exactly where it is and move
  a person into the round. `PLAY_CARD` branches on `isHandover`, and solo — where
  the player *is* the reader and there is nobody to hand to — keeps the
  environmental behaviour.
- **Being read to is not a re-render.** The reader keeps their unfamiliar
  spellings and follows along by ear. Re-rendering would make the two handover
  cards indistinguishable from the other four, and that distinction is the most
  transferable thing in the deck.
- **The scribe is not shown the target sentence.** They type what they hear. A
  scribe reading it off their own phone would be transcription, not a handover —
  and the reader would have nothing to do.
- **The scribe starts from an empty box.** The room is about to watch the
  sentence appear correctly; it should appear from the beginning.
- **The keyboard belongs to exactly one person at a time.** `TYPE` is routed to
  the scribe once the handover lands — not the reader, not a bystander.
- **The mirror is deliberately unhelpful.** No corrections, no spellcheck
  squiggle, no highlighting. The room has to see precisely what came out, and
  the sentence it was meant to be has to stay hidden or the joke is spent early.
- **The round still ends on the reader tapping done.** No matching, no
  threshold, no score — asserted by finishing with text nothing like the target.

## When the helper's phone drops

Both handovers fall back to the version of the same accommodation that needs no
person: the scribe vanishing gives the reader a **working, unmangled** keyboard
back; the aloud reader vanishing lifts the barrier off the passage. Nobody is
punished for a dropped phone — the help stays, it just stops being a colleague.
A bystander dropping changes nothing, and the reader dropping leaves the
handover intact.

## The privacy rule this slice had to amend

"Read it to them" is the one place a non-reader legitimately holds the passage,
and the harness's leak check had to learn about it. Following [[S13]]'s rule —
never weaken an assertion without replacing it — the exemption is narrow and
comes with its own guarantee: the clean passage reaches **exactly one** phone
that is not the reader's, it must be the person holding the card, and no two
phones may hold it at once. Asserted separately, on every view every simulated
participant ever received.

## Also fixed, in the harness

The sim was **never actually reaching either handover** — the coverage rule
decides which barrier each round gets, so waiting for the right card to come up
by chance left those assertions silently never running. Both are now played
deliberately, with a `size >= 8` check that fails if either did not happen.

The harness also had an intermittent failure roughly one run in three, always
the same shape: an emit that never landed, then a cascade of checks failing for
that one reason. A socket emit is fire-and-forget and a simulated phone
mid-churn can drop one, so idempotent events (`DONE`, `END_ROUND`,
`START_REVEAL`, `OPT_OUT`, `END_SILENT`, the two spotlight controls) now re-send
until the state they were meant to produce arrives. `START_ROUND` and
`PLAY_CARD` are deliberately excluded — re-sending those would start a second
round or spend a second card. Three consecutive full runs clean afterwards.

## Verified

296 unit tests: the keyboard moving and the reader losing it, the scribe's input
arriving clean, nobody else able to type, the empty box, the live mirror with
the target withheld then shown, the clean passage reaching the helper and no
other phone (scanned word by word), the reader's screen unchanged and flagged
`listening`, attribution naming the helper, and both disconnect recoveries plus
the two cases where nothing should change. Solo proved to keep the
environmental behaviour for both cards.

Harness green at 1, 2, 5, 8 and 20 with both handovers driven explicitly. In the
browser: a scribe card played on a live butterfingers round, the keyboard moving
to the other phone, and the projector showing *"WHAT IS ARRIVING — The wombat
requires a permit before it may cross."* with the target still hidden.
