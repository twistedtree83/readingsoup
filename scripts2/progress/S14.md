# S14 — Spotlight rounds: volunteers, random draw, coverage selection

Issue: #37 · Branch: `main` · Status: **shipped**

## What shipped

One person reads aloud while the room watches, repeatable up to eight times.
The choice is split down the middle, deliberately:

- **The human owns who reads.** The facilitator taps a name on the projected
  roster, or hands it to chance with *Draw at random*. Participants can tap
  *I'll go* on their phone and surface at the top of the list.
- **The app owns which barrier they get.** Whichever of the six has been
  spotlighted least goes next, skipping anything that reader has already had.

The facilitator sets the count up front against a live time estimate — the
projector shows 1–8 and "About 10 minutes of spotlights" — and can stop at any
point from the clean-passage slide or the lobby.

## Decisions taken

- **Coverage is a greedy least-count rule, not a shuffle.** Random assignment
  shows low contrast three times and never shows the typing one, and the
  catalogue would then name barriers the room never watched. Ties fall to
  `IMPLEMENTED` order so a session replays identically in a test.
- **"Not one they have already had" is a hard filter, not a tie-break.**
  Everyone met a barrier privately in the silent round; re-running it would mean
  the room watches a practised performance instead of genuine first contact.
  Someone who has had all six drops out of the eligible list entirely.
- **The random draw prefers people who have not read yet.** Chance, but not the
  kind that calls the same person twice while somebody else never goes.
- **The count is a plan; eight is the cap.** `SET_SPOTLIGHT_COUNT` clamps to
  1–8, and `START_ROUND` independently refuses a ninth. A facilitator who talks
  past their own plan is not blocked by it, but the room always ends before it
  does.
- **One `makeRound` for solo and spotlight.** The two differ only in who is in
  the seat and where the seed comes from; a round that forked on mode would be
  two implementations of the same thing, drifting apart. Butterfingers arrives
  through the same path, so a spotlight can be a typed round.
- **The reader is never told which barrier they have.** Knowing you have
  Mudsound is knowing which card lifts it. Nothing in any view names it.

## The observer problem this slice had to solve

An "I'll go" button that only participants can see is a **phone-shaped tell** —
the person in the next seat can read your screen. So the button is on every
phone, observers included, and tapping it confirms identically for everyone.

Where the two diverge is the projector: an observer's willingness is dropped in
`view`, not in the reducer. Surfacing it would put an **ineligible name at the
top of the list** — precisely the name the facilitator reaches for — and a tap
that visibly does nothing discloses the role to the whole room at once. Their
own phone still says "You're on the list"; the roster simply never hears of it.

For the same reason the roster names stop being buttons once spotlights end. A
live control that silently ignores you is the same disclosure in a different
place.

## Known gap, for a human

**A facilitator can still tap an observer's name mid-session and watch nothing
happen.** The core has always refused (S12), and every acceptance criterion here
is met, but "pick anyone from the roster" (PRD US 22) and "no one can tell who
chose to observe" (wellbeing rule 2) genuinely conflict at that one tap: a no-op
tells the facilitator, and a silent substitution tells them too. Both bibles are
silent on which gives way, so it is not the runner's call. Everything reachable
without that decision is done — the top of the roster is now guaranteed
eligible, so the affordance the facilitator is actually steered toward never
misfires.

## Also fixed, in passing

- **Session control is host-only, by list rather than by inference.** A
  participant could previously send `START_ROUND` with a `reader` of their
  choosing and put a colleague in the seat. `TYPE` now belongs to the person in
  the seat too.
- **The room code no longer sits under the buttons.** The lobby's left column
  takes the larger share and the code scales to the column, so the one thing on
  that screen a person has to copy off a wall is legible again.
- **The sim waits for views, not for the clock.** Fixed sleeps encoded how fast
  the machine was the day they were written; a twenty-person run under load
  missed them and reported phantom failures. `until()` polls, so the harness got
  both steadier and faster. Below eight participants the spotlight loop runs two
  rounds rather than eight — same code, fewer people, a minute less wall clock.

## Verified

184 unit tests, including coverage of all six barriers over eight rounds at 8
and 20 participants across five seeds, no participant ever repeating a barrier,
and the random draw never landing on an observer at 2/5/8/20. Harness green at
1, 2, 5, 8 and 20, five consecutive runs. In the browser: the plan chooser with
its live estimate, a volunteer's name in red at the top of the roster, *Draw at
random* announcing "Spotlight 1 of 5", the reader's phone showing Soup, and the
clean passage with *That's enough spotlights*.
